BRASS PAWN — APP REVIEW NOTES

1. DEMO ACCOUNT
Not applicable: the app has no accounts. No registration, no sign-in, no
profile, no password — and so nothing to delete. All progress is kept on the
device. The only sign-in anywhere is Apple's own Game Center, used by
Multiplayer; every other mode works signed out.

2. WHAT IT IS, AND FOR WHOM
An offline chess trainer. It plays, grades what you played, and says why the
better move was better. A beginner who plays online gets a result and no
explanation; an engine score of "-2.3" is not one either. This app names the
mistake, says what it cost, shows the move that was there, and then drills the
pattern behind it. For players from beginner to club strength (about 600-2000):
the opponent ladder is built on Stockfish's UCI_Elo, so the engine plays at the
user's strength rather than at full strength.

Modes: Play (engine, coached), Multiplayer (Game Center, on a clock), Board
(free board, two engines, read a position off a photo), Tactics and Rush
(puzzles on a review schedule), Positional and Endgames (drills), Guess the Elo
(judge a real game's strength), Watch (famous games), Today (the day's top
finished games, each at the move where the evaluation turned), Progress (ratings).

3. HOW TO REACH EVERYTHING
No credentials, no sample files, no setup. Every mode is one click from the main
menu. Training modes give five free attempts each per day, with no purchase and
no account, which is enough to see every paid screen working. The paywall is the
card icon at the top right of the main menu, or appears when a day's free
attempts run out; it offers a monthly subscription and a lifetime unlock.
Playing — against the engine and against a person — is free and unlimited.
Today is the TODAY plate on the main menu: a story opens on its key move, Replay
steps through the game, Try it yourself continues it on the free board.
Multiplayer needs Game Center signed in and a second player; if none is found,
that is matchmaking with nobody to match, not an error.

4. EXTERNAL SERVICES
One kind of request of our own: opening the Today screen downloads static JSON
from https://brasspawn.com/media/feed/v1/ (latest.json, then one file per earlier
day as the list is scrolled back; the same files for everybody), through a URLSession with no cookies, no cache and no
identifier. It is never fetched at launch or in the background, and the
server keeps no log of it. The stories in it are written from public facts: the
moves and results of official broadcasts, and Stockfish's evaluations. Nothing
is collected, so the privacy label is unchanged. It uses three Apple frameworks:
GameKit (Game Center, for Multiplayer — moves pass between the two devices, not
through any server of ours), StoreKit 2 (the subscription and the unlock), and
Vision (reading a board off a photograph, on-device). No analytics, advertising,
attribution, crash reporting or third-party SDK of any kind. No AI service: the
chess strength is two conventional engines compiled into the app and running on
the device. Puzzles, drills, games and both engines' networks are bundled.

5. REGIONAL DIFFERENCES
None. Identical features and content in every storefront, no geo-gating.
Localised into 32 languages; the language is the only thing that differs.

6. REGULATED INDUSTRY AND THIRD-PARTY MATERIAL
Not a regulated industry: no gambling, wagering or real-money play, and no
financial, medical or legal content. Third-party material, all open-source or
public domain, credited in the app under About > Licences and in NOTICE.md:
- Stockfish (GPLv3), bundled unchanged with a C interface written for this app
- Reckless (AGPLv3), a second engine, the same
- Lichess puzzle and game databases (CC0 public domain); usernames stripped
- Cormorant Garamond and JetBrains Mono (SIL OFL 1.1)
Because both engines are linked in, the whole application is AGPLv3 and its
complete source is published at https://github.com/minchopm/chess-trainer, which
the app links to from About.

7. PRIVACY
The app collects nothing: no analytics, advertising, tracking, account or
server. Ratings, solved puzzles, review schedule and purchase state stay in the
app's container on the device.

8. CONTENT AND MODERATION
No user-generated content and no messaging. The Multiplayer protocol carries a
move, a resignation, a draw offer, a draw response and a result — there is no
field a person can type into, so there is nothing to report, block or moderate.
Opponents are identified by their Game Center nickname, which Apple supplies.
