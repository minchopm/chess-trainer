A rating is not a measurement of you. It is a measurement of you, against a particular pool, under a particular set of conditions — and change any of those and the number changes with it.

Every rating system in chess works the same way underneath: it predicts the result of your next game from the difference between two numbers, then moves both numbers by however wrong it was. What differs between systems is not the arithmetic — it is who is in the pool, how fast the games are, and how much evidence the number is based on.

That is why the same player is 1500 in one place and 1900 in another without having got better or worse in between. Both numbers are correct. They are answers to different questions.

The one that flatters

Puzzle ratings run high, and it is not an accident.

A puzzle tells you there is something there

The single most valuable piece of information in chess is “this position contains a win”, and a puzzle gives it away before you have looked at anything. In a game nobody tells you. Most of the difficulty of playing well is deciding whether this is a position that rewards two minutes of calculation, and a puzzle has already answered that.

And that it is findable now

A puzzle has a solution that works today, in this position, with the pieces where they stand. A game asks a harder question — what improves my position — which has no verifiable answer and no immediate feedback.

So the gap is structural

Expect a puzzle rating several hundred points above your rating in games, on any platform, at any strength. That is not you being bad at converting; it is the two numbers measuring different things. Neither is the fake one.

The table this page does not have

There is no conversion between Lichess, chess.com and FIDE.

You will find tables claiming there is. They disagree with each other, they disagree with themselves at different rating bands, and they are built from small samples of players who happen to have accounts in two places — which is not a random sample of anybody.

What can honestly be said is directional: online ratings generally read higher than FIDE ratings, and Lichess generally reads higher than chess.com. The size of the gap varies by hundreds of points depending on where you are on the scale and what time control you play, so turning that direction into a number produces a wrong answer with a confident face. If you want to know your FIDE rating, the only way is to play FIDE-rated games.
